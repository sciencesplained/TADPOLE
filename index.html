Helping improve armpit surgery for early breast cancer

The TADPOLE study is a UK research study comparing two types of armpit (axillary) surgery in people with early breast cancer and a small amount of cancer in their armpit lymph nodes.

The study aims to find out whether a more targeted operation can reduce the chance of long-term arm swelling (lymphoedema) without increasing the risk of the cancer coming back.

Thinking about taking part? Already in the study?

Is this study for me?

You may hear about TADPOLE from your breast surgeon or breast care nurse if:

  • You have early breast cancer in one breast.
  • Scans and tests show a small amount of cancer in one or two armpit lymph nodes.
  • You are having surgery as the first treatment for your breast cancer (before any chemotherapy or hormone treatment).

Only your hospital team can confirm whether TADPOLE is suitable for you. You cannot sign up for the study directly through this website.

What would taking part involve?

If you are invited to join TADPOLE and decide to take part, here is what would usually happen:

  1. Discussion with your team – a member of the research team explains the study, answers your questions and gives you written information to take home.
  2. Consent – if you choose to take part, you sign a consent form (on paper or electronically).
  3. Baseline visit – before your operation, we measure your arm, ask about your general health and ask you to complete some questionnaires about your symptoms and quality of life.
  4. Randomisation – a computer allocates you by chance to one of two groups:
    • TAD (Targeted axillary dissection) – only the affected lymph nodes and the first draining (“sentinel”) nodes are removed.
    • ANC (Axillary node clearance) – most or all of the lymph nodes in the armpit are removed (current standard treatment).
    Neither you nor your doctors can choose the group. The TAD group is larger than the ANC group so we can learn more quickly about the targeted operation.
  5. Surgery & usual cancer treatment – you have breast surgery and armpit surgery, followed by any additional treatment (such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy or hormone treatment) recommended by your multidisciplinary team.
  6. Follow-up – we keep in touch for up to five years to check how your arm is doing, how you are feeling and how your cancer treatment has gone. This may include clinic visits, phone calls and questionnaires.

Some extra scans or procedures may be needed if you are allocated to the TAD group, for example an extra ultrasound to locate the affected lymph node before surgery. Your team will explain this in detail.

A more detailed, question-and-answer style description of the study is available on the Information for Participants page.

What are the possible benefits and risks?

Taking part in TADPOLE will not guarantee a personal benefit. However, the information from this study may help improve armpit surgery for future patients with breast cancer.

Axillary node clearance (ANC) is effective but can lead to long-term arm swelling, pain and shoulder problems for some people. Targeted axillary dissection (TAD) may reduce these problems by removing fewer lymph nodes, but it is essential to confirm that this does not increase the chance of the cancer coming back in the armpit.

Your surgeon will talk to you about the general risks of breast and armpit surgery, and about any extra procedures needed for TAD, before you decide whether to take part.

Already taking part?

If you are already enrolled in TADPOLE, your main contact is still your local research team. If you have any worries about your arm, your recovery or your cancer treatment, please contact your breast care team or research nurse as soon as possible.

This website provides general information, but it cannot give personal medical advice or replace discussions with your own doctors.

Who runs and funds the study?

Chief Investigator

Professor Shelley Potter, Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon and clinical trials researcher.

Sponsor

North Bristol NHS Trust is the sponsor for the TADPOLE study. The sponsor is the organisation responsible for the overall conduct of the trial.

Trial coordination

The study is coordinated by the Bristol Trials Centre, University of Bristol.

Funding

TADPOLE is funded by the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR) Health Technology Assessment (HTA) Programme.

Approvals

The study has been reviewed and approved by a Research Ethics Committee and the Health Research Authority. It is registered on a public trial registry (ISRCTN number will be provided once available).

Patient advocates and breast cancer charities have been involved in the design of the study to help ensure that the research focuses on outcomes that matter to patients.

How can I find out more?

If you are interested in TADPOLE after reading this website, please speak to:

  • Your breast surgeon
  • Your breast care nurse
  • A member of your local research team

They can tell you whether the study is open at your hospital and whether you are likely to be eligible. Contact details for local research teams are usually given with your information leaflet.

For general questions about the overall study, you can email the central trial team at tadpole-trial@bristol.ac.uk. Please do not include urgent medical problems in emails – instead contact your usual clinical team.
