Information for people invited to join TADPOLE

This page gives more detail about the TADPOLE study in a question-and-answer format. It is designed to support, not replace, discussions with your hospital team.

Why is this study being done?

Many people with early breast cancer have a small amount of cancer in the armpit (axillary) lymph nodes at diagnosis. The usual treatment is to remove most or all of these nodes using an operation called axillary node clearance (ANC).

ANC is effective, but some people develop long-term arm swelling (lymphoedema), pain, numbness or shoulder stiffness afterwards. These problems can affect day-to-day life and may need ongoing treatment.

A newer approach, targeted axillary dissection (TAD), removes only the nodes known to contain cancer and the first draining (“sentinel”) nodes. This may reduce the risk of lymphoedema and other arm problems. However, we must be sure that it does not increase the chance of the cancer coming back in the armpit.

TADPOLE compares TAD with ANC to find out whether targeted surgery can safely reduce arm swelling and other side-effects, while keeping cancer control at least as good.

Why have I been invited?

You have been invited because your hospital team believes you may meet the main criteria for the study. In general, TADPOLE includes adults who:

  • Have early breast cancer in one breast.
  • Have one or two armpit lymph nodes shown to contain cancer on scan and biopsy.
  • Are having surgery as the first treatment for their breast cancer.

There are also reasons why some people cannot take part, for example more advanced disease, previous surgery to the armpit or certain other medical conditions. Your team will check these details with you.

Do I have to take part?

No. Taking part in TADPOLE is entirely your choice.

If you decide not to take part, you will receive the standard care used at your hospital. Your treatment and the way your team looks after you will not be affected.

If you decide to join the study, you are free to change your mind at any time without giving a reason. This will not affect your ongoing care.

What will happen if I take part?

  1. Discussion and time to decide
    A member of your local research team will explain the study and answer your questions. You will be given written information to take away. You can discuss this with family, friends and your healthcare team.
  2. Consent
    If you choose to take part, you will be asked to sign a consent form, on paper or electronically. This confirms that you understand the study and are happy to join.
  3. Baseline measurements
    Before surgery, we ask about your medical history, measure your arm and ask you to complete questionnaires about your symptoms and quality of life.
  4. Randomisation
    A computer allocates you by chance to one of two groups:
    • TAD group – you have targeted axillary dissection.
    • ANC group – you have axillary node clearance.
    More people will be in the TAD group than the ANC group. Neither you nor your doctors can choose the group.
  5. Surgery and cancer treatment
    You have breast surgery and armpit surgery. Afterwards, you receive any further treatment recommended by your multidisciplinary team (such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy or hormone therapy).
  6. Follow-up
    We follow you up for up to five years with a combination of clinic visits, questionnaires and checks of your medical records. We look in particular at arm swelling, arm and shoulder movement, pain, your quality of life and how your cancer treatment has gone.

Will I need extra tests or visits?

Many of the tests and appointments in TADPOLE are part of usual breast cancer care (for example mammograms and clinic visits).

If you are in the TAD group, you may need:

  • An extra ultrasound scan of the armpit to find the affected lymph node before surgery.
  • Additional injections or scans to help the surgeon find the correct nodes during your operation.

Study visits are usually arranged to coincide with your standard appointments where possible, to avoid extra travel.

What are the possible risks and benefits?

Every operation has risks such as bleeding, infection, pain and stiffness. Your surgeon will talk about these with you as part of your usual care.

ANC removes more lymph nodes and is linked with a higher chance of lymphoedema and shoulder problems. TAD removes fewer nodes and may reduce these risks, but we must be sure it does not increase the chance of the cancer returning in the armpit. That is why this study is important.

You may or may not personally benefit from taking part. The main benefit is that the results will help doctors know which operation is best in the future.

Completing questionnaires can sometimes be upsetting if they make you think more about your condition, but some people find it helpful to reflect on how they are feeling.

What happens to my information?

To run the study, we need to collect and use information about you and your health. This includes basic details such as your name, date of birth, NHS number and contact information, and information from your medical records about your cancer and its treatment.

Your local hospital team keeps this information in a secure system and sends coded study data to the Bristol Trials Centre. When possible, data are “pseudonymised”, meaning they are labelled with a unique study number rather than your name.

We store study data securely and limit access to authorised members of the research team, regulators and auditors. Results are reported in a way that does not identify individual participants.

You have legal rights regarding your personal data. The participant information leaflet from your hospital explains these rights in more detail and tells you who to contact if you have questions or concerns.

How will I find out the results?

When the study is complete and the main results are available, we will:

  • Publish the findings in medical journals and present them at conferences.
  • Share a plain-language summary on this website.
  • Offer to send you a summary of the results, by post or email, if you have said you would like this.

No individual participant will be named in any report.

What if something goes wrong or I change my mind?

If you have any concerns about your care, please contact your breast care team or research nurse straight away. They can advise you and, if needed, arrange a review.

If you wish to stop taking part in the study, you can do so at any time. You can choose whether:

  • You are happy for the study team to keep using information already collected about you; or
  • You prefer us not to use your information from that point onwards, where this is possible.

The complaints procedure and details of any compensation arrangements are described in your hospital’s full participant information leaflet.
